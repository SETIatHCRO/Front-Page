Harold has finished what we proposed that he’d do complete 3B (the Mitsubishi laser/EAM characterization) and 3D (the remainder of the link long-term stability testing).  This email, together with the attached PowerPoint documents, is a summary of those results.

 

Task 3B: Evaluation of the Mitsubishi model #FU653SEA laser/EA modulator component

            Harold has finished characterizing the singe device we have (serial #02ZA02), and the results are summarized in the document “Mitsubishi_A.”  At the linearized bias voltage (~ -1.2 V), the performance of this device is compared to the others we have measured (the four Fujitsu and one NEC) in the final chart of the presentation.  Note that the 5 GHz NF is quite a bit lower and the SFDR higher than for any of the other devices.  It is difficult to speculate whether this is a typical result for Mitsubishi devices without purchasing others and repeating the test, but this is obviously beyond the scope of task 3B (the hours Harold has already spent will deplete the remaining money in that pile).  We should discuss whether you wish to turn us on for further investigation of a larger number of devices, and perhaps at multiple frequencies (I’d suggest 0.5, 5, and 11 GHz).

 

            Under task 3B we had also agreed to repeat a link performance measurement using a different version of the Discovery Semiconductor photodetector—the one with the sliding tab rather than the K connector.  The document “Detector mount” shows the result of a link S21 measurement using the K-connectorized device as we have always done (red curve) vs using the one DSC50S with the sliding tab connector that we have (blue curve).  Notice that, when the DSC50S with the sliding tab was attached to the end of a microstrip line as is, the link performance suffered greatly at some frequencies where the bad ground connection resulted in resonance.  Harold was able to improve the performance using a modified configuration that improved the ground connection, resulting in the green curve.  He feels that with even greater care the single remaining weak (~ 2-dB) resonant suck-outs can be eliminated.
